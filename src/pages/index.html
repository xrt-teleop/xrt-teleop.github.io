We present XRT: an extended reality teleoperation framework that returns a robot's touch to its operator

Drive a robot with your body from a Meta Quest 3, see through its stereo cameras, and feel its fingertip contact as finger-localized vibration or open-ear sound. In our study, we ran two XRT stations together so two operators could share one object through two humanoids.

XRT system

Your body is the controller. The Quest 3's inside-out body and hand tracking drives the robot's arms, hands and neck through closed-form SEW-MIMIC retargeting, with no exoskeleton or hand-held controllers.

XRT system

The robot's touch comes back. One quantizer turns the robot's tactile-skin activation into four contact levels, rendered on the matching finger of a vibrotactile glove, as an open-ear note, or both. No force-feedback hardware, no extra control loop.
